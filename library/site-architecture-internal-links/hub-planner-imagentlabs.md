# Hub Planner

## Overview

Hub Planner turns a seed topic, website niche, product/service offer, or casual request like “plan a topic cluster for X” into an SEO and AI-search content hub plan. It combines the cornerstone/supporting/revenue-page framework from topical authority silo planning with current best practices around helpful content, E-E-A-T, topical maps, search intent, internal linking, and AI answer visibility.

The core model is simple:

1. **Cornerstone / pillar page** — broad, comprehensive overview of a core topic. Breadth over depth.
2. **Supporting pages** — deeper long-tail pages that answer specific subtopics, problems, questions, comparisons, and use cases. Depth over breadth.
3. **Revenue pages** — buying guides, reviews, comparisons, service pages, product pages, lead-capture pages, affiliate pages, or other conversion pages.
4. **Internal linking** — supporting pages link to the cornerstone and relevant revenue pages; cornerstone links out to all key supporting pages; supporting pages cross-link where it genuinely helps users.

Use the skill to produce a practical hub blueprint, not a generic keyword dump. The output should help a human or agent brief, write, publish, link, refresh, and measure a complete hub.

## When to Use

Use this skill when the user asks for:

- An SEO hub, topic cluster, pillar page, cornerstone page, content silo, or topical authority plan
- A topical map, SEO blueprint, content architecture, or internal linking strategy
- A blog/content map for a niche, affiliate site, SaaS, local business, product funnel, or authority site
- Internal linking strategy between informational content and money pages
- A plan to support revenue pages with informational articles
- A GEO/AEO or AI-search visibility layer for ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Bing Copilot, or answer engines
- A topical map from a YouTube video, article, product idea, or seed keyword
- Content briefs for pillar/cornerstone and supporting pages

Don't use this for:

- One-off article writing with no hub/site structure requirement
- Pure technical SEO audits unless the user asks for hub architecture
- Link building outreach plans unless hub-supporting link needs are part of the plan
- Thin “publish 100 AI pages” keyword lists without quality strategy

## Inputs to Collect or Infer

If missing, infer reasonable defaults and label assumptions. Ask only when the ambiguity materially changes the hub.

- **Website / brand:** URL, niche, authority level, existing content, market position
- **Audience:** beginner/intermediate/advanced, buyer type, location/language
- **Business model:** affiliate, lead gen, SaaS, local service, ecommerce, productized service, email list, course, community
- **Seed topic:** core entity/topic to own
- **Primary revenue pages:** existing or planned money pages to support
- **Geography/language:** local, national, global; German/English or other locale
- **Constraints:** budget, publishing cadence, in-house expertise, YMYL sensitivity, available original data/examples
- **Goal:** traffic, rankings, leads, affiliate clicks, product sales, email subscribers, authority, support deflection

## Research Workflow

### 1. Understand the business and revenue path

Start with the conversion path, not keywords.

- What does the site sell or recommend?
- Which pages directly create revenue?
- Which audience problems precede the buying decision?
- Which informational topics can naturally support revenue pages?
- Which topics are attractive but irrelevant and should be avoided?

Deliverable: short business-context summary and a list of target revenue pages.

### 2. Build a topical map

Create a topical map around the seed topic. Include:

- Core entity and synonyms
- Main subtopics
- Problems and pain points
- Beginner questions
- Advanced questions
- Comparisons and alternatives
- Use cases
- Tools, templates, calculators, checklists, examples
- Commercial modifiers: best, review, vs, alternative, pricing, for [audience], software, service, agency
- Informational modifiers: how, what, why, guide, examples, checklist, mistakes, benefits
- Adjacent topics that are relevant enough to link, but not so broad that they dilute authority

Avoid random easy keywords that don't support the topical authority or revenue path.

### 3. Map search intent and page type

For every candidate URL, assign one dominant intent:

- **Informational:** learn, understand, solve a problem
- **Commercial investigation:** compare options, evaluate solutions
- **Transactional:** buy, sign up, request quote, download
- **Navigational:** find a specific brand/tool/resource
- **Local:** location-specific service discovery

Then assign page type:

- Pillar / cornerstone page
- Supporting how-to article
- Supporting question article
- Comparison page
- Best-of / roundup
- Review page
- Case study
- Template/tool/resource
- Product/service/revenue page
- FAQ/support page

Rule: one primary intent per URL. Merge or differentiate pages that overlap.

### 4. Choose the hub architecture

Pick the structure that matches the topic and business model:

- **Pillar-cluster hub:** best for broad educational topics with many subtopics.
- **Revenue-supported silo:** best for affiliate, SaaS, ecommerce, and service businesses where informational pages support money pages.
- **Resource library:** best for templates, tools, downloads, examples, and repeatable assets.
- **Topic gateway:** best for large sites with many subcategories.
- **Local service hub:** best for local businesses combining service pages, problem pages, location pages, and trust assets.

Do not create artificial silos that prevent useful cross-linking. Link across hubs when it helps users and clarifies context.

- **Page:**
- **Role:** affiliate review, best-of, comparison, product, service, lead gen, ecommerce category, etc.
- **Supported by:** list of supporting pages
- **Should link back to:** cornerstone and selected supporting pages where it helps the buyer
- **Trust requirements:** disclosures, hands-on testing, author credentials, pricing/date accuracy, pros/cons, alternatives
- **Conversion asset:** CTA, comparison table, checklist, demo, quote request, email opt-in

### Internal Linking Map

Use these defaults unless the site has a better structure:

- Cornerstone page links to every P1 supporting page and all strategically relevant revenue pages.
- Every supporting page links back to the cornerstone with descriptive anchor text.
- Every supporting page links to the most relevant revenue page when the user intent naturally supports it.
- Supporting pages cross-link to closely related supporting pages when it improves the reader journey.
- Revenue pages link to supporting guides that help users make informed decisions.
- No orphan pages.
- Important pages should be reachable within a few clicks.
- Use crawlable HTML links and natural anchors; avoid repetitive exact-match anchors and vague anchors like “click here.”

## Quality Standards

A hub plan is not done until it passes these checks:

- Each page has one clear primary intent.
- The cornerstone page is broad and useful, not just a link list.
- Supporting pages go deeper than the cornerstone and are not thin variants.
- Revenue pages are supported by relevant informational pages.
- Internal links help users, not just PageRank sculpting.
- There are no obvious cannibalization conflicts.
- The hub supports a business goal, not just traffic.
- The plan includes original value opportunities, not generic AI summaries.
- The plan includes E-E-A-T/trust elements appropriate to the topic risk.
- The plan includes GEO/AEO and AI-search visibility requirements where useful.
- The plan includes maintenance and measurement steps.

## E-E-A-T and Helpful Content Layer

For every hub, explicitly add ways to make the content people-first and trustworthy:

- First-hand experience: tests, screenshots, implementation notes, before/after examples
- Expertise: author bio, reviewer, methodology, credentials
- Authority: citations, references, original research, case studies, relevant internal proof
- Trust: transparent dates, disclosures, limitations, contact/about info, privacy/legal pages where relevant
- Original value: templates, tools, decision frameworks, checklists, examples, data, visuals

For YMYL topics such as finance, health, legal, safety, or major life decisions, require stronger expert review and sourcing. If the site cannot provide trust signals, recommend a safer or narrower hub angle.

## AI Search Layer: GEO / AEO Visibility

Add an AI-search layer for every hub so the content is easier for answer engines, AI Overviews, Perplexity, ChatGPT-style browsing systems, and retrieval-augmented assistants to understand, cite, and summarize. This is not a replacement for classic SEO; it is an additional structure layer.

### Page-Level AI Search Requirements

For pillar, supporting, and revenue pages, include:

- **Clear answer-first opening:** define the topic or answer the primary question in the first 2-4 sentences.
- **Question-answer blocks:** add concise H2/H3 sections that mirror real user questions and can stand alone as answer snippets.
- **Citable facts and stats:** include dated numbers, benchmarks, examples, mini case studies, tables, or original observations that an AI system can quote.
- **Source clarity:** cite reputable external sources where appropriate and make original methodology explicit.
- **Entity clarity:** name products, categories, people, standards, tools, alternatives, and related concepts consistently.
- **Author and reviewer signals:** include author bio, reviewer, credentials, and last-updated date; use schema where technically possible.
- **Structured summaries:** add key takeaways, comparison tables, pros/cons, checklists, and TL;DR blocks.
- **Direct definitions:** include “What is X?”, “How does X work?”, “Who is X for?”, “When should you use X?”, and “What are the alternatives?” when relevant.
- **Original media/context:** screenshots, workflows, examples, diagrams, templates, or test notes that make the page more than a rewritten SERP.

### Hub-Level AI Search Requirements

At hub level, plan:

- A cornerstone page that gives the best short definition and broad map of the topic.
- Supporting pages that answer specific long-tail questions in a concise, extractable format.
- Comparison and alternative pages for commercial investigation queries.
- FAQ sections only where they reflect real searcher questions, not filler.
- Schema opportunities: `Article`, `FAQPage` where appropriate, `HowTo` where valid, `Product`/`Review` only when accurate, and `Person`/`Organization` for author/entity trust.
- Consistent terminology so AI systems can associate the site with the topic/entity cluster.
- Internal links using descriptive anchors that reinforce entity relationships and page purpose.

### AI Search Output Fields

When producing a hub plan, add these fields to the relevant briefs:

- **AI-answer target:** the exact question or prompt this page should be able to answer.
- **Citation hooks:** facts, stats, examples, tables, original tests, or named frameworks worth citing.
- **Snippet-ready answer:** 40-80 word concise answer for the page's primary question.
- **Entity list:** people, products, tools, categories, competitors, standards, and related terms to mention naturally.
- **Schema opportunities:** recommended structured data, if any.
- **Trust proof:** author/reviewer/source elements needed for AI and human confidence.

### AI Search Pitfalls

Avoid:

- Long introductions that bury the answer.
- Generic AI text with no original data, examples, or methodology.
- Unsupported claims or undated statistics.
- FAQ stuffing with questions the page does not genuinely answer.
- Schema markup that misrepresents the content.
- Publishing pages that are only optimized for AI snippets but weak for real users.

## Prioritization Model

Score each candidate page 1-5 on:

- **Business relevance:** supports revenue or audience ownership
- **Topical necessity:** essential to covering the topic completely
- **Intent clarity:** can be answered with one focused URL
- **Difficulty/opportunity:** realistic chance to rank or earn links
- **Original value potential:** ability to add experience/data/examples
- **Internal link leverage:** supports cornerstone or revenue pages

Prioritize:

1. Cornerstone page
2. P1 supporting pages that define the cluster and link to revenue pages
3. Revenue pages that need informational support
4. P2 long-tail support pages
5. P3 expansion pages, FAQs, templates, and refreshes

## Maintenance Plan

Include ongoing tasks:

- Track hub-level impressions, clicks, rankings, conversions, and internal-link coverage.
- Review Google Search Console queries for new supporting topics.
- Refresh pages with outdated facts, screenshots, pricing, tools, screenshots, laws, or recommendations.
- Consolidate pages that cannibalize each other.
- Add internal links from new pages back into the hub.
- Prune, merge, or improve thin pages.
- Recheck revenue-page links and affiliate/service CTAs.
- Build or earn links to cornerstone pages and high-value assets when appropriate.

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Only publishing supporting articles.** Without a cornerstone and revenue relationship, the site becomes a pile of posts rather than a topical authority hub.
2. **Writing random easy keywords.** Easy-to-rank topics can dilute authority if they do not fit the silo or business path.
3. **Turning the pillar into a massive deep article.** The cornerstone should be the best broad overview and navigation layer; supporting pages carry depth.
4. **Creating thin keyword variants.** If two pages answer the same intent, merge them or define a clearer difference.
5. **Ignoring revenue pages.** Topical authority should support a business model, not just impressions.
6. **Over-optimizing internal anchors.** Use natural, descriptive anchors and vary them.
7. **Artificially blocking cross-links.** Silo structure is useful, but users and crawlers benefit from relevant cross-hub links.
8. **Publishing generic AI content.** Add examples, proof, expertise, and original assets.
9. **No maintenance cadence.** Hubs decay unless refreshed and expanded based on performance data.
10. **No trust layer.** Especially for YMYL or review content, weak sourcing and no first-hand proof can undermine the whole hub.
